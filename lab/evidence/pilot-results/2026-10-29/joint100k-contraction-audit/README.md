The priors are genuinely independent uniforms in these physical coordinates: the generator directly draws each parameter from its declared bounds. Therefore prior variance is `(upper-lower)^2/12`, central 90% prior width is `0.9*(upper-lower)`, and logit-normalizing the prior box gives variance `pi^2/3`. No empirical prior sampling is needed. The existing `contraction_logit` is `1 - mean(within-case logit posterior variance)/(pi^2/3)` and reproduces exactly from the saved panel. It uses population variance (`ddof=0`), whose finite-1,000-draw prior-sampling bias is about 0.001 contraction. There are zero outside-bound, exactly-bound or clipped draws, so its 1e-12 tail clipping does not affect this panel. These logit variance reductions should not be confused with physical SD or interval-width reductions.

The posterior **mean** RMSE, divided by the RMSE of always predicting the prior midpoint, is respectively 0.208, 0.094, 0.168, 0.0186, 0.220 and 0.526 in the table's parameter order. Reassigning the same saved posteriors to other cases gives ratios 1.398, 1.411, 1.400, 1.414, 1.400 and 1.306. This control averages exactly over all off-diagonal case pairings, without new random permutations or model calls. Posterior widths stay unchanged, but 90% interval coverage drops from 89.15–90.15% for matched cases to 16.0%, 8.25%, 12.3%, 1.72%, 15.5% and 39.1% when cases are mismatched. A condition-blind prior sampler or constant blindly shrunken posterior would not exhibit this recovery advantage from correct pairing. The earlier saved recovery-correlation field uses posterior medians; this audit explicitly uses posterior means for squared-error risk.

Aggregate Bayesian consistency is also reasonable: mean squared recovery error divided by mean posterior variance ranges 0.903–1.045; mean posterior variance plus variance of posterior means, divided by analytic prior variance, ranges 0.976–1.031. Posterior variance here uses `ddof=1`; finite posterior-mean sampling adds approximately 1/1,000 of posterior variance to squared-error risk. These are descriptive aggregate identities, not certification of conditional calibration or the full joint posterior/copula.

Decay is not uniformly forced narrow. Among 217 cases whose true relative level gap `abs(short-long)/mean(short,long)` is at most 10%, its median SD ratio is 0.985 and median 90% width ratio is 0.979. Among 1,093 cases with relative gap at least 50%, these are 0.208 and 0.214. The 690 intermediate cases have medians 0.546 and 0.552. These are truth-selected descriptive broadness comparisons, not conditional SBC or coverage tests. When the two curve levels coincide, the analytic curve's sensitivity to decay vanishes at fixed levels; near equality gives a natural weak-identification mechanism. Integrating over uncertain nuisance levels can still reshape decay's marginal posterior, so it need not equal the prior exactly.

The same draws identify useful combinations more tightly than individual ridge coordinates. Median SD/prior-SD ratios are 0.049 for rho × eta, 0.032 for xi(0.25y), 0.071 for xi(1y), 0.040 for integrated variance W(1y), and 0.056 for the 0.5–1y forward-variance average. Their prior variances use exact independent-uniform product moments or analytic exponential/exponential-integral moments. The joint simulator uses its analytic initial curve; these are not interpolated latent-curve inputs. In the illustrative near-flat case validation:1962, decay's SD ratio is 1.022 while W(1y)'s ratio is 0.034. That case has no strict-interior plotting claim.
