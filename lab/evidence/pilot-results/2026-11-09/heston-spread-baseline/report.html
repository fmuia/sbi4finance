Which conditional posterior checks fail?

For one fixed set of 54 quotes, compare the network’s draws with MCMC under the same prior, pricing model and Gaussian likelihood. These checks assess the network’s approximation to that posterior. They do not require its mean to equal a generating truth and do not establish that Heston describes actual market prices.

Mean error = |network mean − reference mean| / reference SD ≤ 0.30
Width ratio = network central-90% width / reference central-90% width ∈ [0.75, 1.25]

A pass places the sampling-uncertainty interval wholly inside the allowed range; a fail places it wholly outside; an inconclusive result overlaps a boundary. The evaluator accounts for correlated reference draws and finite neural draws, using approximate family-adjusted uncertainty intervals and two reference block lengths. A combined quantity passes only when both mean and width pass.

All tables and the chart below use the predeclared 60k endpoint. The 55k outcome is also retained; neither endpoint was chosen from the loss curve.

Boundary example: unscored because its reference is unresolved

The first MCMC attempt failed convergence. Its one escalation exhausted the 2,700-second wall limit. The failure and partial chains remain retained. We have no accepted reference for scoring this neural case; its status is inconclusive.

Why can a small mean error remain inconclusive? For broad-case ρ, the estimated error is only −0.024 reference SD, but its sampling-uncertainty interval is [−0.310,+0.263], slightly crossing −0.30. Removing dropout changed a matched 10k control substantially. Continuing to 50k improved widths but left unstable centering. Eight discarded future updates showed that the original 5e-5 learning-rate floor could move broad-case means by .275–.818 reference SD RMS. That prompted one bounded schedule change: 5e-6 for 5k updates, then 5e-7 for 5k. Adam moments, iteration and all saved state were retained.

The annealing fit plus both evaluations completed in 99.86 seconds. At 60k the population rank checks pass 5/5 and 90% coverage is .916–.934, yet both synthetic conditional panels fail. These are repeated development checks on 512 cases, not fresh acceptance evidence. Lower loss and acceptable average ranks can coexist with errors for individual quote surfaces.

How the noise changed, and why smaller noise can demand more precision

Both the original lesson and the current simplified pilot use independent Gaussian price errors:

Yi = Pi(parameters) + σi εi,   εi ∼ N(0,1)

Original synthetic lesson

zi = log(Ki/Fi) / (0.20 √Ti)
sIV,i = 0.003 + 0.007 min(|zi|,2)/2
σold,i = Di Fi Vegaforward(log(Ki/Fi),Ti;0.20) sIV,i Vega is the derivative of option price with respect to decimal volatility. Forward-normalized vega is √T φ(d₁). Original canonical tasks use F=D=1; D×F restores current currency-price units. One volatility point is .01 in decimal volatility.

Current simplified market-profile pilot

hi = (aski − bidi)/2,   σi = c hi,   c=1 in all fits reported here.

Half-spread-as-standard-deviation is a simplifying convention, not a measured Gaussian-error law. The current fits do not infer c. A separate inferred-c likelihood exists, but its network has not been trained.

The intervening τ/vega model, for context
σi = √[(m hi)² + (τ Vegamarket,i)²]

This separated spread-based measurement uncertainty from an inferred discrepancy term in local IV units. It also used frozen market vegas. The current c×h target removes that second component. The new conditional failures therefore occur without jointly estimating two noise sources.

For a controlled counterfactual comparison on the same current 54 contracts, evaluate the original rule in current currency units and divide it by h. The median ratio is 12.89×; the range is 0.23–30.52×. The original rule exceeds h on 48/54 contracts. This is not a measured old-SPY/new-SPX spread ratio, and noise is not uniformly smaller at every deep-wing contract.

With less observation error, informative quotes can produce a much sharper posterior. The inverse problem can become better identified while approximating its conditional distribution demands greater numerical and neural precision. Our tolerance is measured in posterior SD: an unchanged absolute mean error looks larger when that SD shrinks.

A concrete scale in the broad case

Reference v₀ mean≈0.1360258, network mean≈0.1360924. The absolute difference is only .0000666 in annualized variance, but the reference SD is .0000853. That is .78 SD, above the .30-SD tolerance.

Fresh noise augmentation repeatedly perturbs 20k existing clean surfaces; it does not create new physical-parameter locations. The bank audit finds substantial interpolation distances in spread units, even for the available 100k bank. That motivates studying data density but does not prove that learning is impossible or that a larger bank will fix the errors. Sampled pricing refinements and local likelihood curvature agree closely with the reference calculation; they are not a global all-prior precision certificate.

What is volatility of volatility—and is 1.5 unreasonable?

Heston models instantaneous variance V, with instantaneous volatility √V. The parameter convention used here is:

dV = κ(θ − V) dt + ξ √V dW
Time is measured in years; variance and volatility use decimal annualized units. ξ controls random fluctuations of variance and is commonly called volatility of volatility.

There is a real economic counterpart: volatility fluctuates, and volatility derivatives carry information about those fluctuations. Heston’s ξ is nevertheless a model parameter inferred from prices. It is not a directly observed index value.

A local interpretation follows by applying Itô’s rule to σ=√V at V>0. The diffusion term of dσ is (ξ/2)dW. Over a short business-day increment, its local shock scale is approximately ξ/(2√252), in decimal annualized volatility:

0.30.94 volatility percentage points
0.61.89 volatility percentage points

1.85.67 volatility percentage points

This is a local diffusion scale derived from the model, not a finite-day forecast, confidence interval or empirical estimate. Drift, mean reversion and boundary behavior matter, especially at large ξ and small V. For example, a 4.72-point change takes 20% annualized volatility to roughly 24.72% in this local illustration.

There is no universal mathematical ceiling at 1.5. It implies substantial volatility fluctuation under this convention and needs justification in a particular market/task. A posterior pushing above 1.5 is evidence about the model fit under its assumptions, not proof that actual volatility follows Heston with that ξ. In an option calibration we infer a pricing-measure model; interpreting ξ as a directly measured historical volatility statistic requires additional assumptions.

A familiar market analogue is Cboe’s VVIX, derived from VIX options and describing expected volatility of the 30-day forward price of VIX. It is related to volatility-of-volatility risk, but cannot be read off as Heston ξ: the underlying quantity, units and horizon differ.

For mathematical context, the sufficient strict-positivity condition is 2κθ ≥ ξ². Violating it does not make the usual nonnegative Heston variance process invalid; zero becomes attainable. It can matter for numerical schemes. The condition depends on κ and θ together with ξ, so it cannot justify a universal ξ cutoff. See Janek, Kluge, Weron and Wystup, §1.3.3. This report does not establish a typical empirical SPX range for ξ.

What widening ξ established

The old 1.5 ceiling materially constrained that earlier market fit. It did not establish a universally appropriate ξ range, resolve market residual structure, or make a wider-prior neural task comparable with the original example. The earlier spread-only market fit still had RMS residual about 12.3 half-spreads; a higher fitted ξ can be a model's attempt to explain a surface it does not adequately describe. These extra choices should have been highlighted and isolated before interpreting the regression.

A cleaner next comparison

The immediate question should be whether lower noise alone strains the original amortized example. It requires a matched control, rather than another mixed redesign.

  1. Retain the original task and training recipe. Four targets, κ=2, ξ≤1.5, canonical 63-contract geometry, original FlowMatching family, and matched distinct-surface and update budgets.
  2. Vary only a known synthetic noise amplitude. Compare σ=a×σold at a small predeclared set of amplitudes, with separately matched likelihoods and networks. Use a for this synthetic multiplier and retain c for the market-spread model c×h. This tests shrinking noise; it does not claim to reproduce a market spread profile.
  3. Check pricing precision before bank reuse. Tighter noise tightens the numerical requirement. Retain compatible clean labels only where refinement supports them; do not assume zero repricing.
  4. Then introduce market geometry/profile and physical-prior changes separately. A 54-contract, κ=2, ξ≤1.5 control can test the actual-quote setup without the broader dynamics. It needs matching pricing labels: existing free-κ prices cannot simply be relabelled as κ=2.

This is a proposed attribution experiment, not a newly executed fit. The current 60k results remain useful engineering evidence under their own task identity. No training, sampler escalation or Bergomi production was launched to create this report.
